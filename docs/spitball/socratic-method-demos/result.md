# Spitball result — creative ways to demonstrate the Socratic method

**Request:** creative ways to demonstrate the Socratic method
**Value criterion:** SHOW questioning-to-expose so an audience grasps how it
works; enact it, don't lecture about Socrates.
**Method:** 12-agent blind panel mapped the obvious set (9 clusters); candidates
sampled in the band; ranked value × fringe (`0.6·fringe + 0.4·value`).

## The headline finding

**The blind panel answered a different question than the one asked.** All 12
panelists produced *Socratic questioning techniques* — "ask what would break
this," "present two contradictory quotes," "keep asking why." Those are *moves
you make inside the method*, not *creative ways to demonstrate it to an
audience*. The obvious set is the technique list; the band is the missing half —
formats that make an invisible mental process **visible**.

## The final 5 (band, ranked)

| # | Demonstration | v | f | score |
|---|---------------|---|---|-------|
| 1 | **BeliefAutopsy** — live "autopsy" theatre: a volunteer's belief is laid on a table and dissected, each question removing a layer until what's left is examined under a lamp. | 0.80 | 0.62 | 0.692 |
| 2 | **SocraticGPS** — the method as a GPS that only ever says "are you sure?" and reroutes you off every confident road. | 0.62 | 0.72 | 0.680 |
| 3 | **JengaElenchus** — a Jenga tower whose blocks are a belief's premises; each unanswerable question pulls a block until it falls. | 0.78 | 0.60 | 0.672 |
| 4 | **MidwifeBirth** — staged as literal maieutics: the questioner "delivers" the idea the respondent didn't know they were carrying. | 0.72 | 0.64 | 0.672 |
| 5 | **SilentSocrates** — a two-person stage piece where the questioner utters ONLY questions, never a statement, and the audience watches a confident belief collapse in real time. | 0.85 | 0.55 | 0.670 |

**The band has a shape: make the invisible collapse visible.** Autopsy, Jenga,
midwifery, the silent stage — all turn the abstract act of questioning into a
*physical/theatrical event* you can watch happen to a real belief. That's the
move the technique-list panel never made.

> **Honest caveat — the weighting bit hard here.** #2 **SocraticGPS** (value 0.62,
> the *weakest* fulfiller of the five) outranks #5 **SilentSocrates** (value 0.85,
> the *strongest*) purely on fringe (0.72 vs 0.55). SocraticGPS is a cute metaphor
> that only gestures at the method; SilentSocrates actually enacts it. This is the
> `0.6·fringe + 0.4·value` tilt promoting a thin-but-novel idea over a strong-but-
> safer one. **If you're picking one to build, SilentSocrates is the strongest
> demonstration; SocraticGPS is the most "surprising" but the shallowest.** The
> ranker is doing exactly what it's told — surfacing surprise — which is why a
> human reads the value column, not just the rank.

## What the panel made obvious (rejected — 9 clusters, all techniques)

`break_test` (12/12) · `contradiction_pair` (12/12) · `predict_reveal` (9) ·
`counterexample` (7) · `steelman` (7) · `teach_back` (7) · `work_backwards` (6) ·
`deliberate_error` (3) · `five_whys` (3)

Every one is a questioning *prompt*, not a *demonstration*. The injected controls
("ask what would break this," "two contradictory quotes") were both cut — they
ARE the obvious set.

## Negative fringe (the compass the band hugged)

- lecturing ABOUT Socrates (describes, never enacts)
- a bare list of questioning techniques (the obvious set)
- a formal debate (two sides asserting, not questioning-to-expose)
- an info-seeking Q&A (questions for facts, not to collapse an assumption)
- an abstract logic diagram with no live belief tested

The winners sit one step inside that border: real enactment with a real belief on
the line, staged so the *collapse* is the spectacle — close enough to "just a
gimmick" to be non-obvious, disciplined enough to actually show the method.
